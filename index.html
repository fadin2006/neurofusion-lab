NeuroFusion Diagnostic Lab

Combine a doctor's notes, an fMRI slice, EEG and blood markers into one structured result: is there a problem, which brain areas, one area or several, and how severe.

Research prototype with simulated data. The fMRI images, EEG traces and scoring rules are synthetic and transparent. This is not a medical device and must not be used for diagnosis.

    Analyse the large RCT of a neurodevelopmentally informed prevention programme for adolescents (ADAPT, cluster-randomised, about 1,000 pupils aged 13–15)Pre-specified outcome pipeline for ADAPT's questionnaire outcomes, with class-aware models and small-sample corrections.     Risk and protective factors in large adolescent datasetsNormative deviation scores and a validated classifier with honest accuracy, sensitivity, specificity and AUC
    Big data analytics and clear communicationOne report that states presence, areas, extent and severity, with each source's contribution and its uncertainty

    Ideas to improve detection beyond current practice

    • Normative modelling instead of case–control cut-offs. Score each pupil against age- and sex-specific reference curves, so severity is a distance from the healthy norm (Marquand et al., 2016).
    • Low-cost EEG in schools. Four-channel consumer headsets (Muse) recorded in class; channel-agnostic features let models trained on 16–64-channel lab EEG transfer to them.
    • Deep learning on raw EEG with leakage-free validation. Compact networks such as EEGNet, with splits by pupil and by class, never by trial.
    • Learned multimodal fusion with calibration. Replace fixed weights with stacking that is calibrated and can abstain, so uncertain cases go to a clinician.
    • State tracking over time. A particle filter or hidden Markov model follows each pupil's risk state across follow-up waves, rather than a single snapshot.
    • Who benefits. Causal forests estimate individual treatment effects of ADAPT from baseline markers, with replication on held-out classes (Wager and Athey, 2018).
    • Objective alcohol markers. PEth from dried blood spots as a check on self-reported drinking.
    • Fairness checks. Accuracy reported separately by sex and socio-economic group.
